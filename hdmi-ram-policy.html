Why I don’t offer HDMI mods or RAM upgrades

I want every Xbox I work on to be practical, repairable, and true to the experience it was designed for. Some upgrades look exciting, but they often create more trade-offs than real value.

HDMI mods are mostly novelty

External HDMI is more flexible

An external converter lets you use HDMI when you want it and still switch back to a CRT or VGA setup later.

Preserves original video output

Keeping the stock video ports means the Xbox remains compatible with classic displays and easier to service down the road.

High risk, low reward

HDMI mods often require invasive board work that adds complexity but rarely improves the console’s real-world performance.

RAM upgrades rarely justify the effort

Original Xbox memory is already capable of running the games it was built for. Adding more RAM is a tedious and fragile hardware job, and it only becomes useful for a narrow set of emulator-focused builds.

What I recommend instead

Use an HDMI converter

External converters are a safe way to get HDMI output without altering the console. They work when you want HDMI and leave the Xbox unchanged when you want a CRT.

Focus on reliability first

Better cooling, fresh capacitors, drive repairs, and a solid restoration deliver more value for your console than most board-level mods.

Ask before you upgrade

If you have a special use case in mind, I’ll explain whether the upgrade is worth it and recommend the safest option for your Xbox.
